# What these three cells can and cannot identify

## Current result and target gap

The current unit estimates differences and ratios of three displayed survey-weighted percentages reporting AI use. It has no productivity outcome, adoption timing, untreated counterfactual or firm-level joint distribution. Industry association with reported use is not an AI-productivity association, much less an effect. The national domain includes the two sectors; it is not an independent untreated control. No causal coefficient, uncertainty interval, standard error or significance test is estimated here.

The published May 2026 article reports the three selected rates and explains the broadened wording. Source and read extent are archived in the round record. The November 17, 2025 question change prevents treating earlier and later definitions as one homogeneous adoption series. The frozen rounding envelopes quantify only an explicit display-rounding assumption, not sampling or measurement error.

## Proposed causal diagram, not an empirical finding

Let X denote baseline size, industry and workforce composition; U denote unobserved management quality, digital readiness and financing; Y0 denote prior productivity; A denote actual adoption/intensity; Y denote subsequent real output per input; M denote reported AI use; Q denote questionnaire wording; R denote response/observation; and T denote calendar/demand shocks.

Proposed arrows are X→A,Y; U→A,Y; Y0→A,Y; T→A,Y; A→Y; A→M; Q→M; and possibly A,Y,U→R. These are competing assumptions to investigate, not relationships established by the three rates. Conditioning on respondents can introduce selection bias if R has several causes. M measures A imperfectly and changes meaning with Q. The unblocked common causes and absent Y make the adoption effect unidentified by this extract. Adjusting for post-adoption training or organizational changes could remove mediating effects or introduce bias; they are not automatic baseline controls.

## A future design with an identifiable offer effect

A possible future study would randomize an offer of a specified, voluntary AI-support package among eligible consenting employer reporting units. It is only a design proposal: no recruitment, contact, randomization, deployment or data access is authorized or performed by this repository round.

Before any such study, freeze eligibility, assignment unit, intervention content, spillover policy, outcome construction and analysis horizon. A candidate estimand is the intention-to-treat effect of the offer Z on a predeclared real value-added-per-paid-hour outcome at 12 months. Baseline and follow-up accounting definitions, sector deflators, treatment of outsourcing, zeros, attrition and clustering would need resolution using actual data. The current three-cell extract cannot supply these choices or outcomes. Statistical precision and sample-size feasibility are NOT_ESTIMATED.

Random assignment can justify an offer-effect comparison only with correct implementation and explicit handling of attrition, interference and measurement. It does not by itself identify an effect of AI adoption A. A Wald/IV interpretation would additionally require a first stage, exclusion, monotonicity and a well-defined adoption treatment. Training or finance in the support offer could affect productivity directly, making exclusion implausible; therefore no IV effect is promised. Separate adoption-intensity and organizational-complement mechanisms rather than relabeling the offer effect.

## Observational alternative and falsification requirements

If only linked observational panels become legally available, freeze an adoption-date definition and a precise cohort/time estimand before using outcomes. A conditional parallel-trends assumption, no anticipation, common support, stable measurement and explicit spillover assumptions would be needed for a difference-in-differences interpretation. Never-treated and not-yet-treated comparisons are not interchangeable when effects vary over cohorts/time; estimator and weighting would need preregistration.

At least several pre-adoption observations and consistently defined outcomes would be needed to inspect pre-trends and anticipatory responses. Leads, pseudo-adoption dates, pre-treatment outcomes and negative-control outcomes can expose failures; nonsignificant pre-trends do not establish parallel trends. The present cross-section cannot run any of these checks. Selective adoption after a productivity shock remains a live alternative explanation.

Alternative outcomes should distinguish real output, labor input, output quality, costs, revenue and employment. Revenue can change through prices or market power without technical productivity gains; time savings alone are not TFP. Outcome multiplicity, subgroup contrasts, inference clusters and all exclusions must be frozen in a future protocol, not chosen after favorable estimates.

## Measurement sensitivity without invented parameters

For an idealized binary misclassification model with sensitivity Se and specificity Sp, p_observed = Se*p_true + (1-Sp)*(1-p_true). If Se+Sp>1, inversion gives p_true=(p_observed+Sp-1)/(Se+Sp-1). This is algebra, not an identified correction. Sector-specific Se/Sp would require validation data; values outside [0,1] or incompatible implied probabilities would reject the assumed parameter pair. No numerical accuracy parameters or corrected rates are supplied in this round.

Response weighting, coverage exclusions, enterprise-versus-EIN identity, questionnaire interpretation and adoption intensity require separate audits. No policy endorsement, business investment recommendation, productivity effect or completion of ECON-001 follows from this baseline.
